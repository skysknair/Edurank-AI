How Edurank AI works

1. Start with historical cutoffs. Each result has an opening rank, closing rank, year and quota/seat-pool context.

2. Compare your rank to the closing rank. A rank inside the closing cutoff is a historical match; a small tolerance creates a useful borderline band instead of a hard yes/no wall.

3. Show the change over time. The small trend chart compares recent years so students can see whether a branch has been getting tighter or looser.

Why it is different

Instead of one giant “you will get X” answer, Edurank AI shows the college + branch + recent cutoff + gap to your rank. That makes the prediction easier to verify and easier to use while making a preference list.

The current build is intentionally transparent. It does not pretend that a small sample of historical cutoffs is a machine-learning probability model.

Data & verification

The benchmark dataset in this prototype is anchored to published JoSAA opening/closing-rank records and readable historical cutoff tables. JoSAA publishes the current 2026 OR-CR and previous-year OR-CR archive; College Pravesh provides year/round/category/quota formatted cutoff tables used for the embedded examples.

How the fit labels work

Comfortable: your rank is well inside the recent closing-rank history.

Within range: your rank is at or better than the recent closing benchmark.

Borderline: your rank is slightly beyond the recent closing benchmark, so it is worth keeping as a stretch option.

Gap: closing rank minus your rank. Positive means your rank is better than the closing rank; negative means your rank is beyond it.

The fit index is an internal sorting aid, not an admission probability.
